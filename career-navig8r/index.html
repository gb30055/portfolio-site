How do we get more mentors to finish their profile?

Career Navig8r. A mentoring platform where people learn from those who already succeeded in the job they want. I was the only designer, on onboarding and the core product.

The problem

30controls on one setup page

No mentors, no bookings. But becoming a mentor was the hardest flow in the product: 4 crowded steps, a 100-word essay, and one page asking for availability, languages and links all at once.

Mapped before it was drawn

Before any screen, I mapped who signs up, what each kind of mentor has to give us, and in which order, so every step had one job.

One topic per step

Setup became 5 steps: Work Experience, Availability, Mentoring Focus, Plans and Pricing, and Promotion. Each step asks one question, and the left column says why it matters to mentees.

Start empty, end with a clear record. Saved jobs collapse into small cards, so the page stays short as mentors add more.

Before and after. The old Work Experience page asked for every job and a key project in one long form. The new one asks for one job at a time.

The rest follows the same pattern. One question on the left, the answer on the right.

Show only what's needed

The old page showed every control for every day. Now time pickers appear only for days that are switched on, and one slot can be applied to all selected days.

Try it. Switch days on and off, and watch how little is on screen.

A separate path for executive mentors

Senior, certified mentors needed more than the basic setup: proof of certification, specialisms and their own plans. I gave them their own application and setup instead of piling more fields onto everyone else's.

A clear way in. Mentors who already have a profile see two paths side by side, so the upgrade is a choice, not a hidden form.

Before and after. The old pages asked for a count of mentees at every career level and put four offers in one dense grid. The new steps ask for experience in plain words, and give each plan a full card with a clear Offer switch.

Specialisms, not essays. Mentors pick up to six areas from suggestions, so mentees can filter by them later.

Beyond setup

Setup only matters if mentees find the mentors. I also designed the home feed, search and the mentor directory.

Home and search. The feed mixes questions and career milestones, with mentors on the right. Search opens with popular and recent searches before you type.

One search, three kinds of result. People, questions and career updates sit under one query, filtered from the left.

Finding the right mentor. The directory splits Navig8rs from Executive Mentors and filters by sector, job field and level. I tested several card layouts to see how much a card can hold before it gets hard to scan.

Outcome

13 → 6minutes to finish setup

In team walkthroughs, the new flow took about half the time of the old one, with 5 focused steps and at most 8 controls on a screen. This is an estimate from walkthroughs, not usage data.

What I'd do differently

Measure the drop-off before you fix it.

The time savings come from walkthroughs, not usage data. Next time I'd add step-by-step completion tracking before launch, to see where mentors really drop off.
