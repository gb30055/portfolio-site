How do we get more mentors to finish their profile?

The problem

30controls on one setup page

No mentors, no bookings. I took over mentor onboarding from a previous designer, and it was the hardest flow in the product: 4 crowded steps, a 100-word essay, and one page asking for availability, languages and links all at once.

The founder's verdict on the old flow was simple: the process is too long. That became the brief.

Click any screen to see the whole page.

Mapped before it was drawn

Before any screen, I mapped who signs up and what each of them has to give us. Basic users get a short profile. Mentors get their own setup, split so every step has one job.

One topic per step

I split setup into 5 steps: Work Experience, Availability, Mentoring Focus, Plans and Pricing, and Promotion. Each step asks one question, and the left column says why it matters to mentees.

Before and after. The old Work Experience page stacked every job and a key project into one long form. The new one asks for one job at a time.

A short page that stays short. Saved jobs collapse into small cards, and the other steps follow the same pattern: one question on the left, the answer on the right.

Show only what's needed

The old page showed every control for every day. Now time pickers appear only for days that are switched on, and one slot can be applied to all selected days.

Try it. Switch a day on, set its hours, then copy them to your other days with the apply button.

8 controls on screen, growing only with the days you switch on. The old page showed about 30 before you picked anything.

A separate path for executive mentors

Senior, certified mentors needed more than the basic setup: proof of certification, specialisms and their own plans. I gave them their own application and setup instead of piling more fields onto everyone else's.

A clear way in. Mentors who already have a profile see two paths side by side, so the upgrade is a choice, not a hidden form.

Before and after. The old pages asked for a count of mentees at every career level and put four offers in one dense grid. The new steps ask for experience in plain words, and specialisms come from suggestions, so mentees can filter by them later.

Beyond setup

Setup only matters if mentees find the mentors. I designed search and the mentor directory. A junior designer built the mentor cards, posts, comments and other components, with me setting the direction and reviewing each round. Together we tested how much a mentor card can hold before it gets hard to scan.

Outcome

13 → 6minutes to finish setup

Timed in team walkthroughs of the old flow and the new design. Most of the time saved came from fewer fields per step, and from not filling in time pickers for days a mentor doesn't work. It's an estimate from walkthroughs, not usage data.

What I'd do differently

Measure the drop-off before you fix it.

The time savings come from walkthroughs, not usage data. Next time I'd add step-by-step completion tracking from day one, to see where mentors really drop off.
